## Fusion logic
What each genre brings, and how they lock together:

| Genre | Recognisable markers | Role in the fusion |
|---|---|---|
| Slowcore (Low, Codeine, Red House Painters) | 55–70 BPM, space, brushed/sparse drums, hushed close vocal, repetition | **The frame**: tempo, space, vocal intimacy ("intimate") |
| Grunge (Melvins' slow sludge, Alice in Chains' drop-D, Nirvana's strain) | drop-D fuzz guitar, sludgy weight, strained raw vocal | **The texture**: fuzz tone always present, swelling not switching ("raw") |
| Dungeon synth (early Mortiis, Burzum's ambient, the 90s tape scene) | cheap 90s keyboard choirs/strings, modal medieval melody, tape hiss, drumless | **The melody and air**: the modal hook, choir pad, tape hiss ("otherworldly") |

**The cohesion device and signature hook:** one slow, four-note Phrygian figure. The lo-fi keyboard choir and the drop-D fuzz guitar play it **in unison**: the same notes in two genres' timbres at once. Codeine already showed that slowcore and grunge share one tempo (slow + heavy), so no tempo change is needed. Dynamics come from density (the fuzz swells under the choir) instead of quiet-verse/loud-chorus genre switching, which the brief forbids.

Research caveat: I found no documented slowcore × dungeon synth or grunge × dungeon synth releases. Dungeon synth crossovers on record are mostly with black metal and synthwave. Treat the fusion as untested territory rather than an established scene.

## Lyric development — three personas, three iterations

### v1 (songwriter draft)
Conventional four-line verses, with a "coat still hanging where you left it" image, a two-line chorus, and a bridge about breathing in the ceiling.

**Persona A — slowcore purist:** "Far too many words for 64 BPM. Slowcore lives on held vowels and repetition. The coat line is a stock grief image. Cut each line to 2–5 words and let the guitar take the gaps."
**Persona B — grunge vocalist:** "It's all prettily sad, and there's nothing to strain on. Give me one physical, slightly ugly detail and one line at the end I can crack my voice on, *without* making it a separate loud section."
**Persona C — dungeon synth / folklore writer:** "'Otherworldly' needs a mythic undertone, but no swords or dragons. Let the house quietly become a tower or keep. One uncanny number or ritual would do more than any fantasy word."

### v2 (applied all three)
- Verses cut to fragments ("Window, door / Window, door") and the coat image dropped.
- Added the lamp that "comes on at four", a concrete ritual with no explanation.
- Bridge: "The house is a tower / when the heating stops." The mythic register enters through a literal domestic detail.

**Persona A:** "Better. 'Left it on the stair' should repeat — the repetition is the grief."
**Persona B:** "The final chorus needs to *change* or the strain has no reason. Turn the statement into a question."
**Persona C:** "Add the uncanny count: counting stairs in the dark and finding one extra."
**Persona D — Suno prompt engineer (practical check):** "Keep annotations short and in brackets so they aren't sung. The exact title phrase is present ×5. Keep the outro short or the model will invent words."

### v3 (final)
- "Thirteen / then one more": the extra stair.
- Final chorus: "So who keeps the lamp / who keeps the lamp / at four". The statement becomes a question, and that gives the strained vocal its reason.
- The outro returns to "Window, door", the footsteps motif, so the emptiness is still moving at the end.

**Sign-off check:** A: sparse enough ✔ · B: one earned strain point ✔ · C: eerie without fantasy cliché ✔ · D: parses cleanly; phrase present ✔
